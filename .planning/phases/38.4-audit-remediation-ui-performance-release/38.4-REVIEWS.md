### Orchestrator adjudication of new findings
- **Codex MEDIUM [38.4-03]: raw-luminance extrema do not bound composite luminance. CONFIRMED; actionable MEDIUM.** Plan 03 Task 1 (38.4-03-PLAN.md:136, :140) picks `darkestPixel` and `brightestPixel` by the raw pixel's WCAG relative luminance, then composites the tint over only those two pixels. `alphaComposite` blends channels in sRGB space (`src/theme/tokens/surface.ts:350+`), and relative luminance linearises each channel non-linearly, so composite luminance is not monotone in raw luminance across hues. A hand check with a white tint at alpha 0.5 shows this. A pure blue pixel (raw L≈0.072) composites to L≈0.27. A grey pixel with the same raw L composites to L≈0.38. So a saturated pixel that is not the raw-darkest can produce a darker composite than the declared `darkestPixel`. That matters for dark text on Standard-Light glass over the bluish Mesh and Dusk assets. The "real worst-case composite" claim (D-12, must-haves :35-36) therefore does not hold as specified. **Fix (no decision collision; it strengthens D-12):** have `scripts/measure-background-extrema.py` compute the extrema of the *composited* luminance for each regime's tint and opacity (card presentation and chrome, per mode), or declare per-regime composite-extremal source pixels. `--check` should validate those values, and the proof should use them. Also add a fixture where raw and composite luminance order differ.
- **Claude LOW [38.4-03]: interval test may be over-conservative.** This is the mirror image of the Codex finding. The fix above resolves both, since the interval becomes the true composite range. It is not counted separately.
