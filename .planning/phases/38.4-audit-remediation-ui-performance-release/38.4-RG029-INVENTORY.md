## 0. Method and definitions

The corrected proof in `src/theme/tokens/surface.test.ts` composites the card tint (`presentation`
density) and the chrome scrim over BOTH declared extrema of every asset (`darkestPixel`,
`brightestPixel` in `src/theme/backgrounds.ts`, validated against the decoded `.webp` bytes by
`scripts/measure-background-extrema.py --check`). It also requires the foreground luminance to lie
outside the composite interval. In Standard Light both regimes are white `#FFFFFF` @ 0.5. The
decoded worst-case (darkest) composites are Dusk `#94939A` and Mesh `#9DA8B4`.

For the inventory, every `colors.<token>` hit of the six tokens was located with
`grep -rn "colors\.\(statusStable\|statusWobble\|statusDecay\|rogue\|accentText\|danger\)\b" src --include=*.tsx`
(97 hits, tests excluded). Each hit was then classified by its nearest **backing** ancestor.

- **Automated step.** A TypeScript-AST walk found the closest JSX ancestor of the hit that paints a
  backing: `GlassSurface`, `ChromeScrim` or `ShellAppBar`; an overlay (`Sheet`, `Modal`, `ConfirmDialog`);
  or an element whose inline style sets `backgroundColor: colors.surface|surfaceElevated|background`.
- **Manual step.** Hits with no backing inside their own function were traced by hand to every render
  site and read in full.

Where each screen sits:

- Every stack scene is transparent over the shell `BackgroundHost` (`src/theme/navigation-theme.ts`,
  `RootNavigator.tsx:188`).
- The Orrery route forces the `none` background (`focused-route-classification.ts:82`).

| Class | Meaning | Where RG-029 puts it |
|---|---|---|
| **on-glass** | Inside a `GlassSurface` card, a `ChromeScrim` or the `ShellAppBar`. `GlassForegroundScope` applies, so the Standard-Light variant renders here. | Variant required and asserted by the proof |
| opaque | Inside an opaque `colors.surface`/`surfaceElevated`/`background` container or an overlay sheet/dialog. | Not glass. Palette AA tests (`accents.test.ts`, `theme-presets.test.ts`) cover these; exclusion **E-3** |
| fill / border | The token is used as a `backgroundColor`/`borderColor`, not as a text/glyph foreground. | Outside the D-24 set; exclusion **E-4** |
| orrery-overlay | Inside `GlassSurface treatment="orrery-overlay"` on the Orrery canvas. | ADR-149 proof, root palette; exclusion **E-5** |
| bare | No glass and no opaque backing. The text sits directly on the veiled shell art (veil 0.05–0.30). | Not glass, so outside this proof → **finding F-1** |
| n/a | Doc comment, unmounted component, or dev-only harness | — |

## 3. `accentText` — D-24 STOP, resolved by owner ruling D-26 (2026-09-26)

`accentText` renders on Standard-Light glass (§1), so D-24 requires a darker Standard-Light variant for
every curated accent. With lightness alone (hue ±5°, same saturation), **two accents can only pass by
dropping below HSL L 12%**, the plan's near-black STOP floor. The plan stopped here (commit `29592d3`)
and the owner ruled.

**Owner ruling (D-26).** Option 1: accept the sub-12% variants for aurora-teal (`#05312E`) and emerald
(`#083319`), and use the six passing lightness-only variants for the other accents. All eight are now
committed in `STANDARD_LIGHT_GLASS_ACCENT_TEXT` (`src/theme/glass-foregrounds.ts`), keyed by the
resolved accent id (NULL or an unknown id resolves to the package default, `slate-indigo`). The fill
and `onAccent` are not touched. The proof asserts `accentText` for every curated accent plus the
default in every package and mode, and exclusion **E-2** is retired.

relative luminance ≤ 0.027 there.

Per the plan STOP rule, nothing was committed for `accentText` at the stop, and the proof carried it as
exclusion **E-2** until the owner ruled. The options put to the owner (he chose 1):

1. **Accept sub-12% variants** for aurora-teal and emerald (`#05312E`, `#083319`) and the six passing
   variants above. Links in those two accents read as very dark teal/green on Standard-Light glass.
2. **Links on Standard-Light glass resolve to `textPrimary`** for the two failing accents only, or for
   all accents. Hue identity is lost on glass. This is the same mechanism as the secondary override.
3. **Accept a documented exception** for those two accents' links on Dusk and Mesh only. Dawn and Paper
   already pass at their lightest variants.
4. **Allow a small hue/saturation move** beyond the lightness-only rule for those two accents.
   D-24 says lightness only, so this reverses part of D-24.
5. (Forbidden by D-12 unless the owner reverses it) raise `CARD_GLASS_OPACITY.standard`.
