## 3. Concerns

### 1. HIGH — Interrupted switches sample a different camera from the renderer

**Plans:** 03, 13.

Plan 03 explicitly defines `displayedSwitchCamera` below progress 1 using plain `interpolateCamera` (`38.7-03-PLAN.md:128`). Plan 13 adds swirl and breath only to `sampleOrrerySwitchCamera` (`38.7-13-PLAN.md:147`).

Production snapshots the displayed camera when publishing another switch and when enabling reduced motion ([runtime:313](/home/bwales/projects/orbit-app/src/components/orrery/use-orrery-switch-runtime.ts:313), [runtime:371](/home/bwales/projects/orbit-app/src/components/orrery/use-orrery-switch-runtime.ts:371)). The pure runtime sampler also uses plain interpolation at `:115`.

Executed as written, an interruption during swirl/breath starts from the underlying interpolation rather than the camera currently drawn, producing a jump.

**Concrete change:** In Plan 13 Task 1, require one canonical displayed-camera sampler shared by drawing, interrupted publication, reduced-motion retargeting and the pure runtime helpers. Sample the outgoing effects before changing their configuration. Add continuity tests for a second selection and reduced-motion activation at several intermediate progress values.

### 2. MEDIUM — The acknowledgement contract overclaims what one UI callback proves

**Plans:** 04, 07, 08, 09, 13–15.

`38.7-07-PLAN.md:146` says one further UI frame callback establishes that the canvas drew the replacement and no in-flight frame can use the old picture.

Installed Skia records a picture first (`src/skia/core/Picture.ts:22`), then schedules native redraw separately ([RNSkView.h:157](/home/bwales/projects/orbit-app/node_modules/@shopify/react-native-skia/cpp/rnskia/RNSkView.h:157)). Its renderer retains a local reference to the picture it draws ([RNSkPictureView.h:65](/home/bwales/projects/orbit-app/node_modules/@shopify/react-native-skia/cpp/rnskia/RNSkPictureView.h:65)). A later Reanimated callback alone is therefore not evidence of native draw completion.

This is a **verification gap, not a demonstrated use-after-free**: native picture ownership may provide the necessary protection.

**Concrete change:** Amend the canonical contract to distinguish recording acknowledgement, mapper quiescence and native rendering. Require verification of the ownership/refcount guarantees for pictures, images and Paragraph recording; use a supported completion mechanism where those guarantees are insufficient. Add a delayed-native-redraw case instead of testing only delayed ledger acknowledgements.

### 3. MEDIUM — Focus framing uses an estimated card height without a refit path

**Plan:** 11.

Plan 11 frames against `lastCardHeight`, then updates `cardHeight` from layout (`38.7-11-PLAN.md:133–134`). It specifies card repositioning but no framing reconciliation after the real height arrives.

The existing card starts at 180 and later receives its measured height ([OrreryFocusContext.tsx:45](/home/bwales/projects/orbit-app/src/components/orrery/OrreryFocusContext.tsx:45), `:85`). The screen’s framing effect does not depend on card height (`OrreryScreen.tsx:978`). A taller card—particularly after context loads or font reflow—can reduce the reserved region after the person has been framed.

**Concrete change:** Specify a bounded refit while focus framing is still authoritative, or reserve the maximum permitted card height initially. Test first measurement and subsequent context/font-height changes, asserting that the body and moons remain above the card. Preserve user-controlled camera movement after a manual gesture.

### 4. MEDIUM — The no-render guard omits another existing asynchronous update source

**Plans:** 07, 13.

The satellite publisher correction is sound, but unrelated store updates can still render the world. The screen subscribes to the entire system store at `OrreryScreen.tsx:284`. Persistence publishes `"saving"` and later `"saved"`/`"error"` ([orrery-system-store.ts:67](/home/bwales/projects/orbit-app/src/stores/orrery-system-store.ts:67), `:77`). `OrreryWorld` is currently an ordinary component at `OrreryWorld.tsx:167`.

Plan 13’s controlled-promise tests cover satellite loading, photos and semantic tiers (`38.7-13-PLAN.md:175`), but not a persistence completion during the switch. Narrow worklet captures and a constant Picture count do not themselves prevent React commits.

**Concrete change:** Require renderer isolation from unrelated parent/store updates, with stable props/callbacks or another explicit boundary. Extend the mounted-screen guard with delayed successful and failed persistence completing mid-switch.

### 5. LOW — One ambient test contradicts the permitted sky and sun motion

**Plan:** 09.

`38.7-09-PLAN.md:178` says the pulse picture is the **only** layer re-recorded on an otherwise idle ambient tick. The same plan requires sky drift and twinkle at `:149`; D-37 also retains sun pulse. Its acceptance criterion at `:185` correctly says “only ambient layers.”

**Concrete change:** Replace “pulse picture is the only layer” with the allowed ambient-layer set. This is a precision correction; it changes executor behaviour only if the stricter sentence is implemented literally. Freezing the other approved motion would require an **[OWNER-DECISION]**, because it weakens D-36/D-37.

- Both stop conditions for the ring-field fallback are in the plan. If the mechanism proof fails at `RING_FIELD_ANIMATED_MAX`, or the fallback can't animate, the executor stops and escalates under D-32.
- C-M2 removed the self-feedback loop at its source. The card's rect is never a viewport obstacle, and a stability test covers the measure → place → project cycle.
- The D-60 change is covered end to end by the Plan 05 and Plan 11 tests: a second tap on the focused person, a tap on a different person, and the card's "Open Profile".

## Concerns

1. **LOW (does not change execution): stale tap-to-Profile text.**
   - **Where:** RESEARCH Pitfall 8 is marked superseded, but its summary line in the Pattern 3 and "D-47" rows still reads as the old rule. `VALIDATION.md` still describes tap-to-Profile in places. The dossier "Verification Expectations" bullet "projected-size label tiers and the tap-to-Profile threshold" is also stale.
   - **Why it matters:** Plan 05 truths and tasks are correct, so an executor following the plans is fine. A reviewer or verifier reading VALIDATION could expect a Profile-threshold test.
   - **Change:** In `VALIDATION.md` rows 05 and 11, replace "tap-to-Profile" with "tap always focuses; Profile only from the card". Fix the dossier verification bullet the same way.

2. **MEDIUM: the saturated-cap fallback generation can flicker.**
   - **Where:** Plans 04 and 07, the fallback generation that drops lowest-priority pinned entries.
   - **Scenario:** A theme change at a saturated cap momentarily draws many bodies as plain swatches until the queued prepares are admitted over later generations. On All Contacts with ~1,000 bodies that could be a visible multi-generation wash-out.
   - **Change:** Add an acceptance criterion with a bound on generations to full publication (for example ≤ N echoes). Record the observed flicker duration in the Plan 08 or 09 device pass. Or require the fallback to drop only entries not currently visible first.

3. **LOW: the Plan 11 `focusCardRect` is a derived value over `frame` plus `cardHeight`.** The card's measured height arrives via `onLayout` JS state. For one frame the card could be placed with a stale height. Seeding `cardHeight` from the last known height removes the jump. The stability test partly covers this.

4. **LOW: Plan 02 numbering.** "Next free D-NN" is stated as D-63. Plan 08 Task 4 then says "expect D-64", which is correct only if Plan 02 lands first. Say "next free at that time" in both places, or reserve D-63 for the gates explicitly.

## Risk assessment

**MEDIUM.** The plans are sound and ready to execute. The risk is execution, not design:

- The ring-field mechanism at ~1,000 rings is still unproven, but the plan handles that case: it escalates if the mechanism proof fails.
- Plans 04, 05, 07, 09, 13, 14 and 15 are large, with budgets of 170k–300k tokens each. They rely on a 1M-context executor and on the Task-1 commit handoffs to stay recoverable.
- The cap-saturation and ack machinery is intricate. It is well specified and tested, but it is the most likely place for a late-found defect.
