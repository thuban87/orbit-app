**Plain statement (D-49):** the pulses cost about **+3 points of a core on the 6 Pro** (≈ +5 % of the Orrery's idle CPU; ~+1.4 UI thread, the rest garbage collection) and about **+7 points on the 3a** (≈ +12 %, mostly UI thread) — DEBUG. The 3a delta alone is above the locked G6 tolerance of +10 % CPU, so if it held on release the pulses would threaten D-40 there. They do not change presents/s (the ambient clock already presents ~30/s for the twinkle, drift and sun pulse) or the refresh rate. Reported, not dropped. Context (same DEBUG method, Plan 08 screening, before this plan's sky): surf fc bake Size 120 total 56.1 % → now 61 % with pulses off, so the painted sky itself adds ~5 points on the 6 Pro.

A cheaper pulse path is possible without changing the look (see Surfaced items in the 09 return): the per-tick scan of every ring builds a key string per body, which is where the extra GC comes from.

## Nebula port (Lab B) — owner comparison

The owner rejected the haze above: "just gassy looking ... like we're deep inside the nebula, obfuscating the stars".
Cause: Plan 09 ported a soft haze band (Lab A style) and the "as in Lab B" option above only turned that haze up.
Commit `47b1b039` replaces it with a port of Lab B's own nebula (`labb/LabEnvironment.tsx`, `labb-effects.ts`).

What Lab B does, and what V2 now does the same way:
- **Bake once, densities only.** A 768 × 768 seamlessly tiling texture (0.45 texel/dp, so one tile ≈ 1,700 dp; period-8
  lattice) is baked once per Orrery entry. Its channels are densities, not colours: lobe A, lobe B (domain-warped
  periodic fbm, split across the sky by a low-frequency region field) and dust lanes (a finer warped fbm, thresholded
  high). V2 bakes it on an offscreen GPU surface once per (package, mode, size bucket) and copies it to a raster image.
  It is still the sky layer's ledger object (same delayed-ack disposal and teardown), and a System change never bakes.
  The bake now takes 19–34 ms on the 6 Pro DEBUG, down from ~350 ms for the old JS haze.
- **One sky shader per frame.** One texture fetch per pixel. The sky (V2's horizon-to-base gradient) mixes toward lobe A
  and lobe B by density × strength. An ember tint shows where the lobes overlap, a core glow is added on dark themes, and
  the dust lanes darken the clouds. The **far star layer is drawn in the same shader after the clouds**, so it shines
  through them. Then come the corner vignette and a dither. V2's own mid/near stars stay on top.
- **Distance.** The nebula moves 6 % and the far stars 3 % of the world's on-screen motion, scale gently with zoom,
  turn with yaw and shift with tilt. The texture tiles, so no pan ever shows an edge. There is a slow 40 s breathing
  warp; V2's bounded drift is kept. Everything holds still under reduced motion.
- **Tint (H9).** The bake has no colour. The System hue leans lobe B's ink at draw time (`tintStrength` 0.35 now means
  "lobe B leans 35 % toward the System hue"; Lab B leaned it 100 %). G-SKYTINT stays open.
- **Inks.** Lobe A, lobe B and ember are `orreryNebula[0..2]`. The far stars use `orrerySkyStars` (72 / 14 / 14 %). The
  V2 nebula inks are darker than Lab B's (accent and star-palette), so the mix fractions are larger to give the same
  look. Lab B's sky, rendered in CanvasKit with its own inks next to V2's, came out near-identical before the device
  pass.
- **Other themes** use Lab B's own env profiles converted to the V2 inks, at `nebulaStrength` 1. Standard Dark keeps Lab
  B's graphite calm (clouds barely there, no ember). Light themes get low-contrast vellum washes in src-over, with no
  glow and the corners washed toward `orrerySkyStars[0]`. Plan 14 tunes these.

temporary edit, loaded by a relaunch and then reverted; the committed values are "as in Lab B". Lab B was captured
