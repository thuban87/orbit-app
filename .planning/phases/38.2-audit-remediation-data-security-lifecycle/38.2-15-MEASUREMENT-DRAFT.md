# Phase 38.2 Plan 15 — Task 1 measurement draft (D-19 pending)

**Status:** Measurement complete to the extent the current restore path permits. No ingress byte ceiling or acquisition deadline has been enabled. This is the evidence for the owner decision in Task 2, not a sign-off or a plan summary.

## Environment and method

- Physical Pixel 6 Pro, Android debug APK, Hermes, package `com.bwales.orbit`, 2026-09-24. The native APK was built on droid and installed on the Pixel. React Native's *JS Dev Mode* setting was enabled so the `__DEV__` probes were callable.
- The probe writes only schema-valid synthetic backups to app cache, then calls the production restore sequence `readRestoreDocument` → `isEncryptedBackupEnvelope` → `loadBackupForRestore` through a `ready` preview. The source string remains live as in the screen. It records Hermes `usedJSHeapSize` and Android `Debug.MemoryInfo.totalPss` at each stage. These are **point-in-time observed maxima**, not continuous allocation peaks. The standalone `JSON.parse` is run afterward as a secondary measure and excluded from path maxima. Files are deleted in `finally`.
- The synthetic export sizes below came from the real `buildExportManifest` on a migrated in-memory SQLite DB, with 30 KiB synthetic image bytes per photo (40,960 base64 bytes). No live contacts or backup contents were read. The two Drive files were synthetic and deleted after picking; the temporary Pixel Downloads fixture was deleted too. The owner device's backup files were inspected for size only.

## Provider acquisition, separate from copy and parse

These were first picks of unique synthetic Drive-hosted files on the physical Pixel over Wi-Fi. There was no deliberate network throttle. Native debug logs time from request submission to `openInputStream` returning (including queue wait) and time spent streaming to app cache. Drive's internal cache state was not independently inspectable; “first pick” is the cold-access evidence. The local Downloads row isolates provider behavior from cloud download.

At a hypothetical full-file, serial download over **1 Mbps**, the 82.55 MB and 412.16 MB files take **660 s and 3,297 s** before provider overhead; at **5 Mbps**, **132 s and 659 s**. These are **EXTRAPOLATED**, not measured acquisition times or evidence that Drive necessarily waits for a full download before returning a descriptor. They show why a total acquisition deadline of tens of seconds could reject a legitimate cloud file. Throttled physical acquisition remains **BLOCKED (no controlled link throttle available)**.

## Proposal for the owner to decide

The plan's desired guarantees conflict with the current implementation: Orbit can export a 412 MB synthetic photo-rich backup, while this Pixel cannot preview even a 124 MB backup through the existing whole-file Java/JS restore path. There is **no measured byte ceiling that both admits every measured Orbit export and lies within observed restore capacity**.

**Conditional resource-protection proposal:** `MAX_BACKUP_INGRESS_BYTES = 104,857,600` (100 MiB), retain `BACKUP_INGRESS_STALL_MS = 30,000`, and leave `BACKUP_INGRESS_ACQUIRE_MS = null` (no total acquisition deadline). The 100 MiB ceiling is just above the largest successful 101,907,032-byte full-path probe and below the 123,825,650-byte failure. It is only a tested boundary, **not a guaranteed safe threshold for every JSON shape or device**. It would reject photo-heavy backups that Orbit itself can produce (including the 3,000-contact and 5,000-contact examples). The 30 s stall deadline already exists and did not interrupt these copies; a 1 Mbps stream still advances far more often than every 30 s. No acquisition deadline avoids truncating a slow cloud provider before it returns a stream, while the existing supersession/destroy path remains the cancellation mechanism.

**Owner tradeoff:** Approving the conditional 100 MiB ceiling explicitly chooses to strand larger Orbit-generated backups until the restore path is redesigned. Choosing a ceiling above 412,163,749 bytes admits known files that currently OOM before preview. Deferring ceiling enforcement and commissioning a streaming/low-memory restore redesign preserves the current ability to accept large files but leaves the present unbounded-ingress risk until that work ships. Task 3 must not proceed until the owner chooses the risk and values under D-19.
