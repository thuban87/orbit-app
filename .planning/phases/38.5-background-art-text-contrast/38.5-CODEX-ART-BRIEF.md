## What these images are for

They are full-screen backgrounds for a calm mobile app. Text sits straight on top of them, with no card behind it, in
many places. So each image has to stay inside a **brightness band**: very dark images for dark mode, very pale images
for light mode. Inside the band, colour, hue, saturation, soft shapes and texture are all free. The mood comes from hue
and saturation, not from bright highlights or deep shadows.

One image cannot serve both modes (the dark band and the light band do not overlap). So every slot gets **two
different images**: a light version and a dark version.

---

## (a) Format

- **941 x 1672 px**, portrait, **sRGB**, **RGB with no alpha channel**, saved as **PNG**. (The repo transcodes to
  lossless WebP later; do not do that.)
- The band holds over the **whole canvas**, every pixel, edge to edge. Phones crop the sides differently.
- Put the visual interest in the **central 717 px** (x 112 to 829). Only that strip shows on every phone. Foldables show
  only y 248 to 1424.
- Static, no animation. No embedded text chunks needed; they are dropped anyway.

## (b) Brightness bands per package x mode

L\* is perceived lightness, 0 = black and 100 = white. The band is for the art **as you paint it** (the app adds a very
thin veil on top; the checker models that). Stay inside the **design target**, not just the limit: the target leaves
about 2 L\* of headroom for resampling and encoding.

| Package x mode | Hard limit (every pixel) | Grey swatch at the limit | Design target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Galaxy Dark** | **L\* <= 18.5** (Y <= 0.026) | `#2D2D2D` | **L\* <= 16** |
| **Galaxy Light** | **L\* >= 87.1** (Y >= 0.701) | `#DADADA` | **L\* 89 to 97** |
| **Standard Dark** | **L\* <= 21.7** (Y <= 0.034) | `#343434` | **L\* 3 to 19** |
| **Standard Light** | **L\* >= 86.7** (Y >= 0.694) | `#D9D9D9` | **L\* 89 to 97** |

- **Galaxy Dark** is taken verbatim from the 38.5-01 inventory §8 ("Galaxy Dark band"): result `extend` (E-1 covers red
  error text as bare text in Galaxy Dark), art ceiling **L\* <= 18.5**, Y <= 0.026, grey edge `#2D2D2D`, binding
  colour `textSecondary`, design target **L\* <= 16**, checker flag `--exclude-galaxy-dark-danger` (H1 and H2). It is not
  re-decided here.
- **Standard Light** L\* 89 to 97 is the owner-accepted target (D-22).
- In words: Galaxy Dark is deep, near-black space; the brightest wisp is no lighter than a dark grey like `#262626`.
  Galaxy Light and Standard Light are pale all over; the darkest shadow is no darker than about `#E3DDD6` (warm) or
  `#DCE3EC` (cool). Standard Dark is deep all over; the brightest highlight is no lighter than about `#2E2E2E`.
- For coloured pixels the grey swatch is a guide only. A saturated colour can look bright but still be inside the band
  (for example a deep teal or violet at low lightness). The checker measures each pixel exactly.
- Light art: pure white highlights (L\* 100) are fine. Dark art: pure black shadows (L\* 0) are fine.

## (c) The lineup and the busyness tiers

Six slots, each in a light and a dark version, 12 images in all. Galaxy slots are graded by how busy the image is
(D-17); Standard has three slots (D-18).

| Slot id | Tier / idea | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `galaxy-quiet` | **Quiet.** A new image. Not busy, but still obviously galaxy. | Soft cosmic depth: a gentle nebula haze or a faint diagonal drift, a few soft dust points, large calm areas, no focal object. It must read as "space" at a glance even though it is calm. |
| `galaxy-aurora` | **Medium.** Kept from today's set. | Smooth translucent ribbons sweeping in an S-curve, placed to one side (today: the left third), expansive calm areas elsewhere. Owner: "a nice little ribbon that's noticeable but not overpowering." |
| `galaxy-starfield` | **Busy.** Reborn. | A starfield with **stars you can actually see** this time: many points of varied size, soft dust, perhaps faint colour in the dust. The old one was "basically blank, just a black picture in the app." |
| `standard-dawn` | Warm | A warm paper field with soft peach, cream and pale-sand gradient bands, cream toward the top and deeper peach toward the bottom, no focal point. The bands are hue and saturation shifts, not darkening. |
| `standard-paper` | Neutral | Pale linen paper, fine natural fibre grain, soft off-white warmth, almost no tonal change. Fibre contrast stays within about +/-3 L\*. |
| `standard-dusk` | Cool / muted | Dusty mauve and muted lavender, a lighter glow at the upper right drifting diagonally toward the lower left. Carry the drift with a 4 to 6 L\* swing plus saturation, not with deep shadow. |

Cut and **not** to be made: Deep Space, Nebula, Mesh, today's Starfield.

**"Same vein" notes (brief §I).** Today's art is abstract, painterly, soft-edged, calm, with large quiet areas and no
hard lines. Keep that character. Aurora keeps its teal ribbon shape and its one-side placement. Dawn keeps its
peach-to-cream palette; Paper its grain and warmth; Dusk its mauve and lavender hue and its diagonal drift.

**Native tone versus the other mode.** Galaxy's native tone is dark and Standard's is light. The Galaxy Dark and
Standard Light images are the "home" versions. Galaxy Light and Standard Dark are the other-mode versions and get two
routes each (section d).

## (d) Two routes for Galaxy Light and Standard Dark (D-21)

For every Galaxy Light and Standard Dark slot, make **two candidates**:

- **Route 1 (`route1`): the same idea, in the other band.** A pale version (Galaxy Light) or a deep version (Standard
  Dark) of the slot's home image. Start from the leading home-mode candidate of the same slot and repaint it into the
  other band. For Galaxy Light, the owner's seed idea was a pale or "negative" version, for example white or pale field
  with dark stars. (Dark stars on pale art fail the band, so route 1 Galaxy Light also comes as a strict / visible pair,
  section e.)
- **Route 2 (`route2`): a different picture, same busyness tier.** For example, for Galaxy Light: a pastel nebula with
  white star glints (quiet), a pale iridescent ribbon on pearl (medium), a dense field of white and pale-gold glints on
  pastel dust (busy). For Standard Dark: a new deep picture in the slot's family (warm, neutral, cool) rather than a
  darkened copy.

Examples of route 1 for Standard Dark: Dawn as a deep ember-brown-to-plum field with soft warm bands; Paper as dark
charcoal paper with fine grain; Dusk as deep violet-indigo with a subtle upper-right glow.

## (e) Strict and visible-features pairs (D-20)

The strict band leaves some ideas almost invisible: bright stars in Galaxy Dark, a noticeable Aurora ribbon, dark
"negative" stars on pale Galaxy Light art. For those, make **two versions** of the same picture:

- **Strict (`strict`):** every pixel inside the band. Stars and ribbons are expressed with hue, saturation and soft
  glow within the band.
- **Visible features (`visible`):** the features are allowed to break the band so they can be seen. Every other pixel
  stays inside the band. Keep the band-breaking pixels **few and small**: star cores a few pixels across, not large
  bright halos; a ribbon that is visible but not a broad bright sheet. Each candidate's failing-pixel share and its
  largest failing blob are measured and shown to the owner, who decides whether any of it is acceptable.

Which slots get pairs:

| Cell | Pair |
|---|---|
| Galaxy Dark Starfield | strict + visible (brighter stars) |
| Galaxy Dark Aurora | strict + visible (brighter ribbon) |
| Galaxy Light Starfield (both routes) | strict + visible (for example darker "negative" stars on route 1) |
| Galaxy Light Aurora (both routes) | strict + visible (a more visible ribbon; darker parts break the pale band) |

On light art, **white** glints do not break the band (brighter is fine); only features darker than the band do. On dark
art, only features brighter than the band break it.

## (f) Planet-limb exploration (D-16)

Make **one** Galaxy Light candidate showing a soft **pearl planet limb**: a large, pale, pearly curved edge of a planet
entering from one side or a corner, on a pale field, in the Galaxy Light band.

**EXPLORATION, not for shipping unless the owner decides so.** The no-planets rule is relaxed for this one candidate
only. Every other candidate keeps the no-planets rule.

## (g) Rules for every image

- **No** text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, signatures, people, faces, animals, UI, frames, borders, horizons,
  landscapes, buildings, spacecraft, **planets** (except the one section-f candidate) or any recognisable imagery.
  Abstract only.
- No single-pixel specks that break the band, except in a `visible` candidate.
- Soft, calm, large quiet areas; no hard edges.
- Original art made with your image tool. No stock, no copyrighted characters, no external input images other than the
  Orbit background files the prompt names as an edit source.

## (h) Naming and where to write

Every candidate is named:

```
<slot>-<mode>-<route>-<kind>-r<N>.png
```

- `<slot>`: `galaxy-quiet`, `galaxy-aurora`, `galaxy-starfield`, `standard-dawn`, `standard-paper`, `standard-dusk`, or
  `galaxy-planetlimb` for the section-f exploration.
- `<mode>`: `light` or `dark`.
- `<route>`: `base` (a home-mode image), `remaster` (an edit of today's shipped image), `route1` or `route2` (section d).
- `<kind>`: `strict`, `visible` or `explore`.
- `r<N>`: the round number.

Examples: `standard-dawn-light-base-strict-r0.png`, `galaxy-starfield-dark-base-visible-r1.png`,
`galaxy-aurora-light-route2-strict-r2.png`, `galaxy-planetlimb-light-route2-explore-r2.png`.

**Write only the file paths the prompt names, inside your working directory.** Do not create, change or delete any
other file anywhere, and never touch any git repository. Scratch files, if you need them, go in a `tmp/` folder inside
the working directory.

## (i) Self-check (optional; Claude's check governs)

A copy of the project checker sits at `$HOME/orbit-art/38.5/tools/scripts/check-background-art.py`. If your sandbox can
run it (it needs Python with numpy and Pillow):

```
uv run --offline --no-project --with numpy --with pillow python3 $HOME/orbit-art/38.5/tools/scripts/check-background-art.py \
    <file.png> --package <galaxy|standard> --mode <dark|light> [--margin 0.1] [--exclude-galaxy-dark-danger]
```

- `--exclude-galaxy-dark-danger` is for Galaxy Dark only.
- `RESULT: PASS` without `--margin` is the hard limit (H1); with `--margin 0.1` it is the design headroom (H2). A
  `strict` candidate should pass both.
- The printed 8 x 16 map shows where failing pixels sit (top row first).

If you cannot run it, measure L\* yourself with PIL (sRGB to linear, Y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B, L\* from Y) and
report the min, median and max L\* of each output. Remapping tones with PIL after generation, to pull an image into its
band, is fine and expected.

At the end, print one line per output: the file name and its min / median / max L\*.
