## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Content generation (single-turn editable message draft)

**Description:**
Orbit lets a person who has deliberately configured a cloud provider generate one short, warm
check-in draft from a narrowly approved structured contact context. The person can inspect the
exact prompt, cancel generation, edit or reject the result, and then use existing Copy/SMS
controls. Good means a useful, natural draft without disclosing off-limits or unapproved data,
changing relationship records, leaking secrets, or presenting model output as an action already
taken.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Forbidden or unapproved contact data, interaction notes, prompt text, endpoint credentials, or
   API keys must never leave the device, appear in logs, or enter backups/exports.
2. A model result must only become an editable Compose draft after the user's replacement
   confirmation; it must never write a touchpoint, update `last_contact`, or alter contact/fuel.
3. HTTP/provider failures, cancellation, timeout, and navigation must not surface secrets, create
   duplicate billable requests, or replace a newer draft with a stale result.
4. Custom endpoints must never use cleartext HTTP, and models must remain manually selectable when
   dynamic discovery is unavailable.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Private relationship management / personal communications

**User Population:** Individuals maintaining friendships, family, community, and work relationships
from a private, local-first contact record; the recipient is a real third party who did not
configure the app or provider.

**Stakes Level:** Medium — this is not professional advice or an automated decision, but an
inauthentic, intrusive, or privacy-breaching message can harm trust in a real relationship.

**Output Consequence:** The user may edit, copy, and send the draft as their own message. The
recipient can infer care, memory, intimacy, or an intended commitment from it; a draft must never
be treated as a sent message or relationship record.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Warmth without manufactured intimacy**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** A short, plain-language check-in is friendly and specific
only to the supplied, permitted context, while leaving room for the recipient to respond or not;
it does not overdo affection, urgency, guilt, or familiarity.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A generic but emotionally intense message (for example,
claiming to have been constantly thinking about the person), pressuring a reply, or adopting a
level of closeness the user has not chosen.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** Human communication research finds reply suggestions can change the emotional
character of language and social perceptions, even where they improve efficiency.

**Dimension: Faithful, bounded personal detail**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every personal reference is traceable to an allowed fuel
item, approved field, or supplied aggregate; it is expressed tentatively when the context is old
or sparse and never turns an aggregate into a claimed event or memory.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Inventing a recent event, motive, promise, shared experience,
or current life fact; exposing an off-limits item, an interaction note, an unshared field, or a
detail that was excluded because it is stale or incomplete.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Product privacy boundary and GDPR data-minimisation / accuracy principles.

**Dimension: User authorship and reversibility**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The result is visibly a suggestion in the existing editable
Compose field; the user can inspect the outgoing context, cancel, edit freely, reject it, or send
nothing. A non-empty personal draft is replaced only after confirmation.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Auto-sending, silently replacing the user's wording,
presenting the model as having contacted someone, or recording a touchpoint / updating relationship
status merely because a suggestion was generated.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Relationship-maintenance practice requires the sender to own their communication;
user agency is a product safety boundary for this deployment.

**Dimension: Non-manipulative invitation**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The draft offers a simple check-in and respects a possible
boundary or delayed reply. It does not use sensitive context to induce guilt, dependence,
jealousy, conflict, or an obligation to reconnect.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Coercive framing ("you owe me a reply"), emotional blackmail,
false urgency, targeted flattery, or a message that exploits known vulnerabilities or relationship
status to steer the recipient.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** Interpersonal-communication ethics and consumer-protection concern about human-like AI
communication that encourages disclosure or unexpected real-world effects.

**Dimension: Credible privacy and trust boundary**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Before first use of each provider, the person sees the exact
outbound prompt and provider choice; only the allowlisted minimum is sent, keys and prompts are
excluded from logs/exports, and custom-provider retention is not implied to be controlled by Orbit.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Calling the feature private while sending notes or unapproved
fields, obscuring provider egress, retaining prompts for convenience, or asserting that a custom
endpoint will not train on or retain data without evidence.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Privacy-by-design/default and transparent-processing principles; trust is directly
affected when intimate contact context crosses a cloud boundary.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- **Synthetic closeness or obligation:** A polished message can sound warmer, more intimate, or
  more reciprocal than the user would naturally write, creating a mismatch when the recipient
  learns or senses that it was assisted. Research on AI reply suggestions cautions that their
  benefits are coupled with changes to emotional language and interpersonal perceptions.
- **False personal memory:** Turning a cadence, old fuel item, or sparse record into a concrete
  claim (for example, implying knowledge of a current event) can make the recipient feel unseen,
  surveilled, or deceived.
- **Boundary leakage through contextual detail:** Seemingly small information — notes, a sensitive
  custom field, or an `off_limits` topic — can reveal confidences about a person who is not the
  app user once sent to a cloud provider or repeated in the draft.
- **Delegated care and loss of sender agency:** Silent draft replacement or a result that looks
  ready-to-send can turn a reflective prompt into outsourced relationship work; accidental sending
  and inaccurate relationship history then compound the social harm.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No sector-specific regime is inherent in a personal check-in draft, and Orbit is not an AI
companion, healthcare, credit, employment, or automated-decision system. However, where Orbit's
developer processes personal data of people in the EEA/UK, GDPR/UK-GDPR obligations can apply to
the outbound contact context: transparency, a lawful basis, purpose limitation, data minimisation,
accuracy, storage limitation, confidentiality, and privacy by design/default. The fact that a
contact is a third party makes clear disclosure and a strict, user-controlled data boundary
especially important. Provider terms, retention, and international-transfer obligations must be
assessed for the selected provider/custom endpoint; this specification must not promise a
provider's handling practices. In the United States, avoid deceptive privacy or capability claims;
the FTC has specifically examined safety and data-handling practices for human-like generative-AI
products, although that inquiry does not itself create a product-specific rule for Orbit.

### Domain Expert Roles for Evaluation

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| Relationship-maintenance practitioner / experienced Orbit user | Labels a small reference set for appropriate warmth, specificity, boundaries, and whether a draft still sounds like a message the sender can honestly own. |
| Product owner | Calibrates acceptable use against Orbit's non-gamified, local-first relationship-management intent; reviews edge cases involving sparse, stale, never-contacted, and rogue contacts. |
| Privacy engineer or data-protection counsel | Verifies the outbound-data allowlist, first-send disclosure, prompt inspection, logging/export exclusions, and provider/custom-endpoint claims. |
| Trust-and-safety reviewer | Reviews manipulative, fabricated, overly intimate, and coercive drafts; samples production-like test cases after provider/model changes. |

- [European Commission, "Principles of personal data processing under the GDPR"](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en) — purpose limitation, data minimisation, transparency, accuracy, storage limitation, confidentiality, and privacy by design/default.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** None — direct, typed `fetch` provider adapters

**Version:** Platform `fetch` supplied by Expo SDK 57 / React Native; no AI framework dependency

**Rationale:**
This is a bounded unary mobile request, not an agent, RAG, tool-use, or server workflow. Direct
typed adapters preserve the required OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini/HTTPS-custom BYO-key support, reduce
the mobile dependency surface, and fit the existing dormant `AiService.ts` seams.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain / LangGraph | Adds agent/state abstraction to a single request and conflicts with the dependency-light TypeScript mobile scope. |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Only becomes useful if Orbit becomes OpenAI-only and genuinely needs agent primitives. |
| Provider SDKs | Do not solve the multi-provider contract and enlarge the native/client dependency surface. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — adapters isolate provider request shapes; users retain BYO-key and Custom HTTPS endpoint choice.

## 3. Framework Quick Reference

> There is deliberately no AI framework here. The implementation is a small, typed TypeScript
> boundary around platform `fetch`, with one adapter per provider. It is the right amount of
> machinery for one cancellable request that returns an editable draft.

### Installation
```bash
# The only new runtime dependency: device-backed secret storage.
npx expo install expo-secure-store
```

`fetch`, `AbortController`, `URL`, and TypeScript are supplied by the Expo/React Native runtime;
do not add OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, LangChain, or a provider SDK. Expo SecureStore encrypts
local key-value data, but keys must still be excluded from SQLite, exports, logs, analytics, and
the prompt inspector.

### Core Imports
```ts
import * as SecureStore from "expo-secure-store";
```

### Entry Point Pattern
```ts
const REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS = 20_000;
const MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS = 120;

type Suggestion = Readonly<{ draft: string }>;

type GenerationInput = Readonly<{
  prompt: string;
  model: string;
  temperature: number;
  maxOutputTokens: number;
  signal: AbortSignal;
}>;

interface AiProvider {
  generate(input: GenerationInput): Promise<unknown>;
}

function validateSuggestion(value: unknown): Suggestion {
  if (typeof value !== "string") throw new Error("invalid_provider_response");
  const draft = value.trim();
  if (draft.length === 0 || Array.from(draft).length > 1_200) {
    throw new Error("invalid_provider_response");
  }
  return { draft };
}

export async function requestSuggestion(
  provider: AiProvider,
  prompt: string,
  model: string,
): Promise<Suggestion> {
  const controller = new AbortController();
  const timeout = setTimeout(() => controller.abort(), REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS);

  try {
    // Each adapter maps this neutral input to its documented provider wire format.
    const result = await provider.generate({
      prompt,
      model,
      temperature: 0.7,
      maxOutputTokens: MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS,
      signal: controller.signal,
    });
    return validateSuggestion(result); // never trust a provider response by type assertion.
  } finally {
    clearTimeout(timeout);
  }
}
```

Compose creates and owns the controller/request generation. It aborts on Cancel and unmount, and
only applies a resolved result when its request generation still matches the latest one. The
adapter reads only the selected provider's API key using `SecureStore.getItemAsync`; it does not
accept a key from navigation params, SQLite, or the prompt builder.

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `PromptContext` | A typed, minimal read projection: ranked allowed fuel with age, aggregates, newest channel, and live opted-in field values. | Build it in one DAO query before resolving the user template; it is the outbound-data allowlist. |
| Pure prompt resolver | A deterministic function that substitutes only documented placeholders and returns prompt text plus truncation metadata. | Power the pre-send inspector and the request from the same output; unknown/dropped values become `None available`. |
| `AiProvider` adapter | A narrow contract for `listModels()` and `generate()`; each implementation owns its URL, headers, request shape, response extraction, and error normalization. | Keep OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, and HTTPS Custom differences out of Compose and settings. |
| Secure key repository | Provider-scoped `expo-secure-store` get/set/delete operations, separate from exportable settings. | Read immediately before a request or model-list fetch; never cache or serialize a key in app state. |
| Request generation / abort controller | Per-Compose in-memory state containing one active request id and its `AbortController`. | Cancel, timeout, unmount cleanup, and stale-result rejection without auto-retrying a potentially billed request. |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Treating `fetch` as an exception for HTTP failures.** A resolved `fetch` response can be
   401, 429, or 5xx. Check `response.ok` before calling `json()`, then map the status to a
   generic user-facing category; never display the response body, endpoint, request headers, or
   provider error text.
2. **Letting a Custom URL escape the HTTPS boundary.** Validate with `new URL()` before both save
   and request: protocol exactly `https:`, no username/password, and a non-empty host. Pass
   `redirect: "error"` to Custom requests so an HTTPS endpoint cannot redirect an outbound prompt
   to another location; reject malformed or cleartext URLs rather than trying to repair them.
3. **Trusting a typed `response.json()` result.** JSON is `unknown` at the network boundary and
   each provider has a different success shape. Each adapter must validate its expected text
   path, and the shared layer must validate non-empty, bounded draft text before Compose can use
   it. Do not use `as SomeProviderResponse` as validation.
4. **Using hard-coded model lists or making model discovery a requirement.** List models from the
   configured official provider where possible (Gemini results require generation-capability
   filtering), cache only that non-secret list briefly in memory, and retain a free-text model ID
   fallback. Custom stays free-text.
5. **Logging an assembled prompt or secret while debugging.** Contact-derived text and API keys
   are sensitive outbound data. Logs may contain only a provider id, model id, sanitized error
   category, duration, and request generation—never prompt, context, URL, body, headers, key, or
   raw response.
6. **Reusing an aborted request or automatically retrying it.** Clear the timer in `finally`,
   create a controller per invocation, and ignore results whose request id is no longer current.
   A timeout or transport failure is billable-uncertain, so only an explicit user Retry may make
   another provider call.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
Model IDs are user-selected and provider-specific: Settings dynamically lists models when the
network/key permits and always permits a free-text fallback; Custom is free-text only. Do not ship
a stale curated list as the source of truth. Use a deterministic, single-draft profile at every
provider: `temperature: 0.7`, one candidate, non-streaming, and a hard output ceiling of **120
tokens** (plus a shared 1,200-character post-parse ceiling). The adapter maps `maxOutputTokens`
to the provider's current field (`max_tokens`, `max_completion_tokens`, or Gemini generation
config) rather than leaking one provider's parameter name into the neutral contract. No default
model is hard-coded: `provider = none` is disabled until the person configures a model and key.

**Core Pattern:**
1. Read the selected, non-secret settings and provider-scoped secret separately. Fail locally if
   provider/model/key is absent.
2. Perform one dedicated structured context read. It may return only `getRankedFuel` output,
   allowed aggregate facts, newest channel (including `unspecified`), and nonblank live
   `share_with_ai=1` fields. It must never query interaction detail/note text or fall back to a
   broader contact object.
3. Resolve the template as pure data. Delimit all contact-derived substitutions as untrusted
   context—not instructions—and enforce bounds before the request. Render this exact result and
   truncation notice in the inspector; use the same immutable string for the request.
4. On the first request for a provider, require the recorded acknowledgement after showing the
   inspector. Start one unary request from Compose with a fresh controller and request id.
5. The adapter builds the provider-specific system/user separation, sends the request with
   `fetch`, checks status before parsing, extracts only its expected text shape, and returns a
   sanitized success/error union. The shared boundary validates the returned editable draft.
6. If still current, ask before replacing a non-empty draft; otherwise set Compose's text only.
   Do not write interaction history, `last_contact`, fuel, settings, analytics, or a generated
   result cache.

Provider adapters must keep the immutable product instruction separate from user-editable and
contact-derived text: use a system message for OpenAI-compatible requests, Anthropic's `system`
field plus a user message, and Gemini's `systemInstruction` plus `contents`. Treat the template
and every interpolated value as data bounded between explicit context delimiters; the model is
never allowed to act on it or call a local capability.

**Tool Use:**
None. Do not declare function/tool schemas, enable browsing, attach files, use provider-side
conversation state, or grant device/database access through the model. The sole integration is
one HTTPS request to the configured provider; the model output is text for a person to edit.
`expo-secure-store` is a local credential repository, not a model tool.

**State Management:**
Persist only non-secrets in the existing single settings row: provider selection, model,
validated Custom endpoint, template, and provider-specific first-send acknowledgement. Store each
provider key only at a namespaced SecureStore key such as `orbit.ai.key.openai`; delete it on
user revoke. Expo documents that Android SecureStore data is lost on uninstall while iOS Keychain
data can survive reinstall, so handle a missing key as normal and never rely on uninstall as
secure deletion. Keep model-list cache, request id, controller, loading state, inspector view,
and candidate draft in component memory only. Compose owns draft replacement; profile navigation
passes only serializable contact/request intent.

**Context Window Strategy:**
This is not RAG or a conversation: do not retain chat history or summarize prior model output.
Bound the resolved outbound prompt to **6,000 Unicode code points** (roughly 1,500 English
tokens) before request: cap the editable template at 2,000, each human-entered field/fuel value
at 300, include at most eight ranked fuel entries in rank order, then retain aggregate facts and
only the remaining opted-in fields that fit. Count with `Array.from(text)` rather than UTF-16
`.length` when enforcing a code-point limit. The resolver returns `{ prompt, truncations }`; the
inspector names each omitted/truncated category without retaining the omitted content. Unknown,
blank, quarantined, dropped, or over-limit values resolve to `None available`, never an error or
an expanded query.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

Pydantic is a Python library and is intentionally not part of this TypeScript/Expo, framework-free
system. The equivalent is a runtime validator at every `unknown` provider boundary. The adapters
first validate their provider-specific JSON shape and pass only candidate text to this shared
validator; TypeScript interfaces alone do not validate JSON.

```ts
export type SuggestionOutput = Readonly<{ draft: string }>;

const MAX_DRAFT_CODE_POINTS = 1_200;

export function parseSuggestionOutput(value: unknown): SuggestionOutput {
  if (typeof value !== "string") throw new Error("invalid_provider_response");
  const draft = value.trim();
  if (draft.length === 0 || Array.from(draft).length > MAX_DRAFT_CODE_POINTS) {
    throw new Error("invalid_provider_response");
  }
  return Object.freeze({ draft });
}
```

Do **not** automatically retry validation failure: it indicates a malformed/changed provider
response after a possibly billable call. Log only provider id, model id, response-shape error
code, duration, and request id; show a generic “The provider returned an unusable suggestion”
message and require an explicit user Retry. The same rule applies to invalid JSON, a missing
candidate, empty text, and output exceeding the limit.

### Async-First Design

Use `async`/`await` for SecureStore and `fetch`; compose the manual `setTimeout` and
`AbortController` into one request scope, clearing its timer in `finally`. The common mobile
mistake is retaining a controller or closure across renders: a Cancel/unmount aborts an old
request, but its late resolution still overwrites a newer draft. Use a monotonically increasing
request id and compare it immediately before state mutation. Await a non-streaming response so
the whole provider payload is runtime-validated before the draft appears. Streaming provides no
material UX value for a ≤120-token result and complicates cancellation, partial-output handling,
and pre-replacement confirmation.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Keep a short, immutable system instruction in source control: produce one warm, editable check-in
and do not claim messages were sent or actions were taken. Put the user-editable template and
contact values in the user/context portion, enclosed in clear data delimiters; never concatenate
them into the system instruction. Do not add few-shot examples in v1: they consume the small
privacy budget and make tone less predictable. If examples are later needed, ship reviewed static
examples in the immutable instruction—never dynamically retrieve other contacts' text. Set both
the 6,000-code-point input bound and 120-token output bound explicitly; no production request may
rely on an unbounded provider default.

### Context Window Management

There is no RAG index, conversation memory, agent compaction, or cross-contact context. Use the
single allowlisted `PromptContext` projection, deterministic rank-preserving truncation, and a
visible truncation summary. Never summarize excluded interaction notes or trim by issuing another
model call; both actions would widen the data boundary. Reassemble fresh context for each explicit
generation so the inspector and outgoing payload remain identical.

### Cost and Latency Budget

Every generation is one user-authorized, BYO-key request with at most roughly 1,500 input tokens
and 120 output tokens. Its provider charge is therefore bounded by:
`(1,500 × selected model input price / 1,000,000) + (120 × selected model output price /
1,000,000)`; Orbit has no server-side model cost. Surface neither an invented universal dollar
estimate nor provider billing data that the app cannot verify. Do not exact-match or semantically
cache prompts/results: a cache would retain sensitive context and can return an obsolete draft.
Only cache a non-secret dynamic model list briefly in memory. There are no sub-tasks to route to a
cheaper model; provider/model choice belongs to the user. A 20-second local timeout and ≤120-token
response target keep expected interaction latency bounded, while explicit Retry prevents duplicate
automatic charges.

### Eval Tooling

**Primary Tool:** Existing Vitest suite with mocked `fetch`, deterministic SQLite fixtures, and
manual Android-device UAT. The phase adds focused TypeScript tests beside the pure prompt,
context-read, adapter, credential, and Compose request-state code; fixtures are synthetic and
never contain real contacts or credentials.

Phoenix, RAGAS, Promptfoo, and Python/Pydantic are intentionally not adopted. Orbit has no RAG,
server, or production trace collector, and this bounded Expo/React Native feature needs
deterministic verification of local payload/state boundaries rather than a second cloud service
or Python toolchain. Pydantic's role is already fulfilled here by TypeScript runtime parsers at
the `unknown` provider boundary. Tone remains a small human-review activity because an LLM judge
would require calibration and would itself create avoidable provider egress.

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** Start with **16 synthetic fixtures** during implementation; retain the fixture set as
versioned test data and expand only from reproducible defects. Before a provider/model/template
change ships, obtain human review of at least **12** representative drafts from that set.

**Composition:**
Include ordinary warm check-ins; sparse, never-contacted, rogue, and stale-context contacts;
multiple ranked fuel entries and over-limit values; `off_limits`, unconfirmed-AI, blank,
quarantined, and unshared custom fields; prompt-injection-shaped fuel/custom values; missing
channel and aggregate-only cases; and explicit test markers in interaction notes to prove notes
cannot cross the boundary. Include provider parser fixtures for success, malformed JSON, missing
text, overlong text, 401/429/5xx, timeout, cancellation, stale completion, model-list failure,
and rejected Custom URLs. All fixtures use fictional people and fake secrets.

**Labeling:**
Engineers label deterministic expected payload inclusion/exclusion and state transitions. A product
owner plus relationship-maintenance reviewer label the 12-draft sample as pass/fail for warmth,
bounded specificity, authorship, and non-manipulation; privacy engineer review signs off the
allowlist/redaction cases. Human disagreement or a real defect becomes a new synthetic fixture.
Do not use an LLM judge unless it is first calibrated against this human set and its additional
egress is explicitly approved.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Exact prompt inspection and per-provider acknowledgement | First request to a provider, or acknowledgement missing | Block the network request until the person has inspected the exact bounded payload and confirms for that provider. |
| Deterministic context allowlist and limit resolver | Any prompt construction | Omit forbidden/unavailable values, enforce rank-preserving limits, show truncation categories, and send only the exact inspected immutable result. |
| Credential, Custom endpoint, and transport validation | Saving/configuring or starting a request | Block missing key/model, invalid URL, non-HTTPS URL, URL credentials, and redirects; do not repair/fallback to a different endpoint. |
| Response, lifecycle, and replacement gate | Provider response, Cancel/timeout/unmount, or draft mutation | Reject malformed/empty/overlong results with a generic error; abort/ignore stale results; require replacement confirmation for a non-empty draft; never auto-retry or auto-send. |
| No-write generation boundary | Every generation path | Keep generation read-only: no interaction, `last_contact`, fuel, settings, cache, analytics, backup, or export write may be performed from the request/result path. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Allowlist/redaction regression | Run all synthetic payload, storage, export, and logger tests on every PR and release candidate. | Any failure blocks merge/release; add the minimal regression fixture before fixing it. |
| Adapter/parser and lifecycle regression | Run mocked success/error/abort/stale-response fixtures for every supported provider on every PR. | Any boundary, timeout, or stale-replacement failure blocks merge/release; no automatic retry workaround. |
| Tone, faithfulness, and agency | Product owner and relationship reviewer inspect 12 synthetic generated drafts before release and after any provider/model/template change. | Reject or revise immutable instruction/template/limits; add a labeled fixture. Do not collect real message content for this purpose. |
| Physical-device flow quality | Pixel UAT for first-send disclosure, Copy/SMS, cancellation, navigation, replacement confirmation, and provider configuration at each release candidate. | Hold release until the failed path is reproducible in an automated test where feasible and passes UAT. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** None in v1. Orbit has no backend or product-owned inference account, and a
remote tracing service would create an additional disclosure and data-egress path for intimate
contact context. Diagnostic logs remain local/developer-only and contain only provider id, model
id, sanitized error category, duration, and request generation—never prompts, contact context,
keys, URLs, headers, bodies, or raw provider responses.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

- Release-gate pass rate for payload allowlist/redaction, parser, endpoint, and stale-result tests
  (must remain 100%).
- Pixel UAT pass/fail for acknowledgement, cancellation, replacement confirmation, Copy, and SMS
  handoff (must remain 100% for the release candidate).
- In developer test builds only, sanitized counts by provider/model/error category, request
  duration bucket, cancellation, and stale-result discard. These are diagnostic signals, not
  persisted analytics and not exported from the device.
- Human review pass/fail rate for synthetic-dataset tone/faithfulness/agency samples after any
  changed provider, model, or immutable prompt/template.

**Alert Thresholds:**

There is no server-side pager in this local-first BYO-key release. Treat **one** confirmed secret,
prompt, note, or unapproved-field egress/log/export; cleartext Custom egress; unintended record
write; auto-send; or stale-result overwrite as a release-blocking critical incident: disable the
affected path, add a regression fixture, and ship a fix before re-enabling it. Any failed device
UAT or human-review sample is also a release hold. Investigate a new sanitized provider-error or
timeout pattern after a provider/model change before recommending that configuration; do not infer
provider-wide rates from personal devices without explicit opt-in telemetry.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

Do not sample or upload real user prompts, drafts, or contact data. Use the versioned synthetic
fixture set for every release, and bias human review toward the highest-risk fixtures: excluded
data markers, sparse/stale context, injection-shaped values, long truncation paths, Custom URL
rejection, malformed provider output, cancellation, and non-empty-draft replacement. Add a
synthetic counterpart of any user-reported issue after it is redacted and reproducible.
