### 2. Genuine toss-ups, and why I chose

**a) SHELL before THEME (near-tie).** The edge runs both ways: **SHELL-13** wants "the status bar
integrates with the active theme", while **THEME-09/10/11** are app-wide sweeps ("**All** screens draw
icons through a centralized semantic icon registry"; "Functional content meets strong AA-equivalent
contrast in **every** supported theme/mode combination") that are cheapest once the final chrome
exists. I put SHELL first because (i) SHELL-13's dependency is satisfiable against v1.0's already
shipped theme-token store, whereas THEME-09/10/11/12 have no equivalent fallback for chrome that
doesn't exist yet; (ii) **THEME-03** requires theme-critical preferences to "restore before first
render", which wants the shell's boot path settled; (iii) THEME-13's storage migration should run
once, over a final preference set. Reversing this pair is defensible and cheap.

**b) CAPT before HIST — the sharpest call in the roadmap.** I chose CAPT → HIST → GRP on:
**HIST-14**'s verb ("Interactions **gain** optional duration … shown only when present") reads as an
additive upgrade to a form that already exists; **CAPT-15** owns the vocabulary migration
(quality → Tone, six channels → three) that **HIST-11/12** then render and edit; and CAPT-08/09/10
define Channel/Direction/Connected/Tone/Allow-AI, which HIST-11 enumerates verbatim.
**The counter-case is real:** HIST-10/11/12 declare the *canonical* Interaction Detail/Edit route,
and **CAPT-13** ("**History-originated** logging prefills that day") reads as CAPT consuming an
existing History surface, mirroring **HIST-15**. If HIST ran first, the residual forward edge is
CAPT-07's "Duration under More Options"; if CAPT runs first (my choice), the residual forward edges
are HIST-15/CAPT-13's entry point and HIST-14's duration column. I judged CAPT-first to leave the
smaller and more additive seam. **Flag this pair for the owner — it is a coin-flip that changes
three phase positions.**

**c) PROF before ORRC/ORRS (weak forcing).** The Orrery cluster's dependencies close at CARDV — it
needs SHELL, THEME-06/12, KNOW-05, DASHQ, and (for ORRS-06's "searchable virtualized avatar grid")
CARDV-01/05/06. It has *no* dependency on PROF/CAPT/HIST/GRP, so it is placeable anywhere from 29 to
34. I placed it right after PROF because **PROF-11** establishes the Gravity presentation vocabulary
("a named tier plus a wide-range size-coded sphere, never editable") that **ORRC-03** ("size is
subtly influenced by derived Gravity") and **ORRC-15** ("Gravity context") would otherwise invent
independently — two phases minting a Gravity vocabulary in parallel is a conflict waiting to happen.
Secondary reason: the Orrery is the milestone's highest technical risk (Skia render loop; per
PROJECT.md, perf claims are physical-Pixel-only), so it should not sit in the last three slots.
**Alternatives considered:** ORRC/ORRS at 29–30 (before PROF — de-risks earlier, loses the Gravity
ordering) or at 33–34 (after GRP — keeps PROF→CAPT→HIST→GRP contiguous, but strands the highest-risk
rendering work next to the AI phase).

**d) Your Week placed at 36 — mid-sequence, not in the deferred tail.** This is the most contestable
placement. Its hard dependencies close at GRP: **DASHQ-14** relocates birthday presentation to it,
**DASHC-02** ships its header entry point, and its blurb names Group Event rollups (← GRP) and
heatmap-aggregation reuse (← HIST). I put it *before* AICFG because **AICFG-17 freezes the wire
format as the milestone's final schema act**, and Your Week is the only deferred slot with a
substantive feature payload — the one most likely to introduce a portable preference or a rollup
entity. Anything it adds after the v4 bump would either be unbackupable or force a follow-on format
bump that AICFG-17's wording forbids. I also placed it after COMP (35) because a week view that
lists who is due will want a Message action, and **COMP-14** makes Compose deep-link-ready and
origin-aware. **Alternative:** keep all four deferred slots contiguous at 37–40 and accept a
follow-on migration if Your Week turns out to need one.

**e) Deferred-slot order: Your Week → (AICFG) → Settings → Onboarding → Hardening.**
Settings & Personalization "consolidates the preference/admin seams exported by" the other phases —
and **AICFG-01** ("a credential-management escape hatch remains while AI is off"), **AICFG-11**
("**Settings** can inspect the whole resolved system/prompt"), **ORRS-09** ("from the switcher and
Settings") and **DASHC-09** ("both Archived entry points (overflow + **Settings** row)") mean the
seams it consolidates include AICFG's. So Settings must follow AICFG. Onboarding "against the real
product" must follow Settings (it teaches first AI setup per **AICFG-12**). Hardening is an audit
pass over everything and is necessarily last.

### 3. Derived obligation the requirements do not state

**AICFG-17 makes the three post-AICFG deferred slots schema-frozen.** Because the v4 bump is
declared the milestone's final schema act, Phases 38–40 (Settings, Onboarding, Hardening) inherit an
implicit constraint: they may consolidate, present and audit, but any *new* entity or *new* portable
preference they introduce owes its own migration and a format-5 bump. This should be written into
their stub contracts before they are interrogated. (Phase 36 Your Week is placed before AICFG
precisely to exempt it from this.)

## Phase Details

### Phase 22: App Shell & Navigation

**Goal**: The user can move through the whole app inside one predictable navigation frame, and can
start any of six capture actions from wherever they are standing.

**Depends on**: Nothing in this milestone (first phase; builds on v1.0's shipped surfaces).
SHELL-13's "status bar integrates with the active theme" is satisfied against v1.0's existing theme
token store and re-verified after Phase 23.

**Requirements**: SHELL-01, SHELL-02, SHELL-03, SHELL-04, SHELL-05, SHELL-06, SHELL-07, SHELL-08,
SHELL-09, SHELL-10, SHELL-11, SHELL-12, SHELL-13, SHELL-14, SHELL-15

**Success Criteria** (what must be TRUE):
1. User can move among Dashboard, Orrery, Backup/Restore and Settings from a persistent bottom bar, and each tab returns them exactly where they left it; re-tapping the active tab first closes transient UI, then returns to that tab's root.
2. User gets identical, non-surprising results from Android Back and the on-screen Back on every screen — transient layers dismiss first, a finished edit never replays, and Back from a Profile returns to the surface they actually came from.
3. User can expand a consistently placed FAB into a labeled six-action speed dial in fixed order; contact-specific actions preselect the contact whose Profile they were on, and global ones open the shared picker.
4. User can find anyone through one search-first picker ordered Favorites → recently relevant → alphabetical, with archived people appearing only on explicit search and snoozed people still selectable.
5. User who Quick Logs sees a success snackbar with Undo **only** when the write committed, and an error with Retry when it did not; leaving a focused workflow with unsaved changes always offers Discard / Keep editing.

### Phase 23: Theme & Visual System

**Goal**: The user can make the whole app look the way they want, and every screen stays legible,
consistent and motion-safe in all four theme/mode combinations.

**Depends on**: Phase 22 — THEME-09/10/11 are app-wide sweeps over chrome that SHELL-01 (bottom nav),
SHELL-08 (speed dial), SHELL-10 (picker modal) and SHELL-13 (status bar / title treatment) introduce;
THEME-03's "restore before first render" needs SHELL's final boot path.

**Requirements**: THEME-01, THEME-02, THEME-03, THEME-04, THEME-05, THEME-06, THEME-07, THEME-08,
THEME-09, THEME-10, THEME-11, THEME-12, THEME-13

**Success Criteria** (what must be TRUE):
1. User can choose Galaxy or Standard independently of Light / Dark / Follow System, watch every change preview live, and relaunch straight into the right theme with no wrong-theme flash.
2. User can set an accent and a bundled background per theme package, and each package remembers its own accent, background and appearance choices.
3. User can read functional content at strong AA-equivalent contrast in every theme/mode combination, and can distinguish every relationship status with color vision disabled — each status has its own silhouette glyph.
4. User's OS reduced-motion and text-scaling preferences are honored app-wide: nonessential motion stops or simplifies (including inside the Skia render loop), and text reflows rather than truncating or shrinking.
5. User's theme, accent and background choices survive a backup and restore onto a fresh install.

### Phase 24: Contact Knowledge Foundation

**Goal**: The user has one place that holds everything worth remembering about a person — and can
find any of it by typing roughly what they remember.

**Depends on**:
- **Phase 22** — the Memory/relationship/custom-field editors are focused workflows governed by SHELL-06/07 (chrome hiding, Discard/Keep) and reached through SHELL-08's "Memory" speed-dial action; SHELL-10's picker supplies the contact link in KNOW-05.

**Requirements**: KNOW-01, KNOW-02, KNOW-03, KNOW-04, KNOW-05, KNOW-06, KNOW-07, KNOW-08, KNOW-09,
KNOW-10, KNOW-11, KNOW-12, KNOW-13, KNOW-14, KNOW-15, KNOW-16

**Success Criteria** (what must be TRUE):
1. User sees first-class fields, custom fields, structured relationships and typed Memories together in one grouped "Things to Remember" surface, featured and current information first.
2. User can record a Memory of a built-in or custom-labeled type with optional note, link and meaningful date, pin it, and mark it outdated without deleting it — and can restore a deleted one from Recently Deleted.
3. User sees the current Last Talked About and Current Location on the Profile, can drill into the full backlist, edit a historical entry, and promote an old value back to current.
4. User can search from the Dashboard and find people by Memory label/value/note, relationship name and custom-field content — with typos tolerated and internal metadata never matched.
5. User finds every item's AI permission OFF unless they turn it on, sees the sparkle only where it is on, and sees Off Limits marked as a topic to avoid independently of any AI setting.
